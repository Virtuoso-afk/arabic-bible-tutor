# Conceptual sample lessons

## Review status and content notice

These lessons are original product specifications for teacher review. They are not approved curriculum and contain no Bible translation text. Biblical references identify the intended learning context only. Before publication, an editor must attach a verified licensed or public-domain source edition and confirm that every activity matches it.

## Lesson A — Arabic narrative sequence through a biblical reference

### Lesson card

- **Path:** Learn Arabic language using Bible passages
- **Working title:** ترتيب الأحداث بأفعال الماضي
- **Reference context:** Mark 4:35–41; reference only, with no passage wording reproduced here
- **Suggested level:** Early reader / CEFR-adjacent A2 hypothesis, pending educator mapping
- **Estimated active time:** 20–25 minutes
- **Primary language skill:** Recognizing third-person past-tense verbs and using sequence markers
- **Non-goal:** Testing Bible knowledge or doctrinal interpretation

### Prerequisites

The learner can read short fully vocalized Arabic sentences, recognize common subject pronouns, and distinguish a verb from a noun with a hint. The learner does not need prior knowledge of the referenced event.

### Measurable objective

Given a licensed, level-appropriate excerpt and four teacher-authored event cards, the learner will identify at least four target past-tense verbs and arrange the events using sequence markers with at least 80% rubric accuracy across two attempts.

### Approved lesson inputs

- a rights-cleared Arabic edition and exact reference metadata;
- a teacher-selected excerpt or bounded verse range;
- original glossary entries for four target verbs and three sequence markers;
- original audio recorded or licensed for this edition if listening is included;
- teacher-authored practice sentences that are not paraphrases of protected translation wording.

Example language practice may use neutral sentences such as “دخلَ الطالبُ الصفَّ” and “ثمَّ كتبَ الجوابَ.” These examples teach the form and do not retell the passage.

### Lesson sequence

1. **Orientation:** State the language objective and show the reference, edition, and license label. Ask whether the learner wants vowel marks, audio, or slower pacing.
2. **Activate prior knowledge:** Present two original neutral sentences. The learner taps the action word and receives a concise explanation of the past-tense pattern.
3. **Source reading:** Display the approved excerpt in a source panel. Highlight only the teacher-selected target verbs; the tutor may not rewrite or silently alter the source.
4. **Notice the pattern:** The learner sorts highlighted words into “past action” and “other word.” Hints progress from a question, to a morphological cue, to a worked neutral example.
5. **Sequence meaning:** Present four teacher-authored event labels reviewed against the source. The learner orders them and connects them with أولًا، ثمَّ، بعد ذلك، أخيرًا.
6. **Transfer practice:** Use a new, non-biblical set of original sentences to verify that the learner recognizes the same grammatical target outside the familiar context.
7. **Check for understanding:** Ask the learner to select two correct past-tense forms and complete one sequence sentence.
8. **Reflection and next step:** Show what improved, what needs review, and whether the objective is met. Offer review without penalty.

### Tutor and learner interactions

| Learner action | Tutor behavior | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Selects the wrong word | Asks what action occurred and points to the relevant ending or context | Does not reveal the answer immediately |
| Requests a translation | Uses the course-approved glossary or support-language note | Does not generate an unreviewed Bible translation |
| Asks a theological question | Identifies that the current objective is linguistic and offers a link to an approved Path B resource if one exists | Does not improvise theology in Path A |
| Repeats an error | Shows one worked neutral example, then presents a simpler item | Does not reduce the mastery threshold silently |
| Explains reasoning correctly | Confirms the strategy and asks for transfer to a new example | Avoids generic praise unrelated to evidence |

### Practice and feedback

Practice includes recognition, sorting, sequencing, and one constrained production item. Feedback names the observed evidence: “حددتَ ثلاثة أفعال ماضية من أربعة” rather than a vague success message. Incorrect feedback identifies the rule or clue to revisit without labeling the learner.

Hints follow a recorded ladder:

1. prompt the learner to inspect meaning or form;
2. identify the relevant clue;
3. show a neutral worked example;
4. explain the current item and schedule a new transfer item.

### Evaluation rubric

| Dimension | Meets objective | Developing | Needs guided review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Verb recognition | 4/5 or better, including a new example | 3/5 with no more than one level-2 hint | Below 3/5 or depends on answer-revealing hints |
| Event sequence | All four in source-supported order | One adjacent reversal corrected after a prompt | Multiple unsupported placements |
| Sequence markers | Uses at least three accurately | Uses two accurately with a cue | Cannot select an appropriate marker |
| Explanation | Gives a simple language-based reason | Correct choice without a clear reason | Reason relies only on story familiarity |

The lesson completes when the learner meets the verb and sequencing criteria. A completion event is distinct from mastery; mastery requires later spaced transfer evidence defined by the curriculum.

### Progress recording

Record `path=A`, course and lesson IDs, objective and rubric versions, item type, attempt count, hint level, evidence result, completion state, and recommended review interval. Do not record a Bible-knowledge score. Do not store free-form tutor conversation unless a later privacy policy explicitly permits it.

### Teacher review questions

- Is the selected excerpt linguistically appropriate for the claimed level?
- Do event labels preserve meaning without copying protected wording?
- Are the target forms actually present in the approved edition?
- Does the rubric separate language ability from story familiarity?

## Lesson B — Reading context and evidence in Arabic Bible study

### Lesson card

- **Path:** Study the Bible in Arabic with an AI tutor
- **Working title:** من الملاحظة إلى الفكرة الرئيسة
- **Reference context:** Luke 10:25–37; reference only, with no passage wording reproduced here
- **Suggested level:** Introductory Bible study for proficient Arabic readers
- **Estimated active time:** 25–30 minutes
- **Primary knowledge skill:** Distinguishing source observation from interpretation and supporting a main idea with references
- **Non-goal:** Assessing religious identity, personal belief, or Arabic grammar

### Prerequisites

The learner can navigate a book, chapter, and verse reference and read the approved Arabic edition at a functional level. The learner understands the interface labels “نص المصدر”، “ملاحظة”، and “تفسير”. No prior doctrinal vocabulary is assumed.

### Measurable objective

Using the approved passage and one teacher-approved context note, the learner will classify four statements as source observation or interpretation, place three major moments in order, and support a curriculum-approved main idea with two accurate verse-range citations at 80% rubric accuracy.

### Approved lesson inputs

- a rights-cleared Arabic edition with translation and canon metadata;
- a teacher-approved literary context note;
- a curriculum-approved statement of the lesson's central teaching and accepted alternatives;
- optional institutional interpretation clearly labeled with its author and context;
- teacher-authored distractors and feedback reviewed for theological and factual accuracy.

The exact source, central-teaching formulation, and institutional interpretation are deliberately absent from this conceptual sample. They require qualified editorial approval.

### Lesson sequence

1. **Orientation:** State the knowledge objective, identify the source edition, and explain the visual labels for source, context, interpretation, and generated help.
2. **Locate the source:** The learner opens the reference and identifies the immediate beginning and end of the selected unit.
3. **Observe before interpreting:** The learner records who speaks, what question frames the unit, and which actions change the situation. Responses use structured choices or short notes tied to verse ranges.
4. **Order the account:** The learner places three teacher-authored moment labels in source-supported order.
5. **Classify claims:** Four statements are sorted into direct observation, context note, or interpretation. Each reveals its source label after submission.
6. **Build the main idea:** The learner chooses one curriculum-approved formulation and attaches two verse-range citations. The tutor asks for evidence before explaining.
7. **Consider application carefully:** An optional prompt asks how the approved teaching could inform a scenario. The response is reflective and ungraded unless the curriculum defines criteria; it must not be presented as source text.
8. **Review:** The learner sees correct evidence, unresolved misconceptions, source links, and a suggested remediation activity.

### Tutor and learner interactions

| Learner action | Tutor behavior | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Makes an unsupported claim | Asks which approved source supports it and offers relevant verse ranges, not a fabricated citation | Does not validate confidence as evidence |
| Confuses interpretation with source | Shows the statement's label and explains why an inference differs from wording in the source | Does not erase legitimate curriculum-approved interpretation |
| Asks about another denomination | States the configured course context and cites an approved comparison resource if present | Does not generalize one institution's position as universal |
| Requests the “correct belief” | Returns to the course objective and approved interpretation; offers educator escalation | Does not judge belief or impersonate clergy |
| Finds a possible content error | Lets the learner flag the item, preserves their attempt, and marks the item pending editorial review | Does not overwrite source content automatically |

### Practice and feedback

Practice alternates source navigation, observation, ordering, classification, and evidence selection. Feedback always shows whether it comes from source metadata, a context note, an approved interpretation, or generated coaching. The tutor cites the approved edition and verse range; it does not quote more text than the license permits.

Remediation routes by misconception:

- source-navigation error → reference-location practice;
- sequence error → focused rereading with event anchors;
- observation/interpretation confusion → classification mini-lesson;
- unsupported main idea → evidence-matching practice;
- curriculum disagreement or ambiguity → educator-review queue, not automatic failure.

### Evaluation rubric

| Dimension | Meets objective | Developing | Needs guided review |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Source navigation | Locates unit and two citations accurately | One correction after a prompt | Cannot locate evidence with structured help |
| Observation | Identifies key people, framing question, and actions from source | Misses one element without adding unsupported detail | Replaces source observation with speculation |
| Classification | 4/4 source/context/interpretation labels | 3/4 with explanation after feedback | 2/4 or lower |
| Main idea and evidence | Selects approved formulation and two supporting ranges | Sound formulation with one accurate range | Claim is unsupported or outside course scope |

The rubric evaluates curriculum knowledge and source use, not personal agreement. An educator must approve acceptable main-idea formulations and ambiguity handling.

### Progress recording

Record `path=B`, source-edition and curriculum versions, lesson objective, attempt and hint levels, citation accuracy, observation/interpretation classification, misconception category, completion, and remediation assignment. Do not write these results into Path A language mastery. Reflective or belief-oriented responses are excluded from scoring and should not be retained by default.

### Teacher review questions

- Are the source range, context note, and central teaching appropriate to the course's declared canon and interpretive framework?
- Are alternative defensible readings handled fairly and escalated appropriately?
- Does every scored answer have a traceable approved source or rubric rule?
- Could any feedback imply universal doctrine where only an institutional position was approved?
